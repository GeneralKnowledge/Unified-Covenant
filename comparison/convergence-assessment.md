# Convergence assessment (Phase 5.2)

**Purpose:** Test whether proposed **points of convergence** among Judaism, Christianity, and Islam are historically and theologically defensible—without synthesizing a new religion or editing [THE_UNIFIED_COVENANT.md](../THE_UNIFIED_COVENANT.md).

**Evidence base:** [history/01](../history/01-before-the-branches.md)–[04](../history/04-the-emergence-of-islam.md); [evidence-matrix.md](./evidence-matrix.md) (including disagreement register §2 on crucifixion layers).

**Phase 5.3:** Source traceability and outcome wording were audited in [convergence-source-audit.md](./convergence-source-audit.md) (registry `comparison-phase-5-3-source-audit`). **No compatibility outcome category was changed**; several propositions were **narrowed** where the chronicle does not support post-70 rabbinic or Nicene claims directly.

**Outcomes (provisional project categories):**

| Outcome | Meaning |
|---------|---------|
| **Genuine convergence** | Core proposition affirmable without contradicting normative positions on the matter tested |
| **Qualified convergence** | Common formulation possible if differences stay explicit |
| **Translation-dependent convergence** | Agreement needs shared vocabulary that may distort original claims |
| **Unresolved** | Chronicle/matrix insufficient |
| **Irreducible disagreement** | Same-sense conflict; no justified harmonisation |

## 1. One God and divine unity

### A. Proposition

Humanity’s Abrahamic traditions can affirm **one ultimate God** (not a polytheistic pantheon) in their respective scriptural and early-historical layers—**without** requiring identical metaphysics of divine nature. Phrases such as “uncreated ground of moral responsibility” are **philosophical gloss** (essay-style), not a single formula all three texts use in the same sense.

### B. Evidence by tradition

| Tradition | Evidence type | Content (period / stream) |
|-----------|---------------|---------------------------|
| **Judaism** | Scripture + **Religious tradition**; historical **Widely accepted interpretation** | YHWH as God of Israel; stress on exclusive devotion in many post-exilic texts; monolatry → monotheism **Contested** pace ([Ch.1](../history/01-before-the-branches.md), [Ch.2](../history/02-from-israel-to-judaism.md)). **Post-70 rabbinic** monotheism: **attributed** via Cohen/Grabbe (Ch.2 bib.), **not** narrated in chronicle (`rabbinic-judaism-post-70-preview`). |
| **Christianity** | **Religious tradition**; **Ch.3** historical | One God of Israel; early **exalted Christ-devotion** without Nicene Trinity in 1st c. ([Ch.3](../history/03-the-emergence-of-christianity.md)). Mainstream later: Trinity/incarnation (**outside Ch.3**). |
| **Islam** | **Religious tradition**; **Ch.4** | *Tawḥīd*; rejection of *shirk* ([Ch.4](../history/04-the-emergence-of-islam.md)). |

### C. Historical relationship

**Structural similarity** (one-God rhetoric); **Shared inheritance** (Israelite/biblical idiom, Late Antique monotheism); **Different interpretation** (what “one God” entails—Trinity vs. tawḥīd vs. Jewish divine simplicity). Trinity is **not** shared inheritance as doctrine.

### D. Compatibility test

**Qualified convergence** on a **minimal** claim (“one ultimate God, not many gods of a pantheon”). **Irreducible disagreement** if the proposition requires **same metaphysics** (Trinitarian incarnation vs. Islamic/Jewish rejection of divine sonship/partnership). Matrix: [§ A1](./evidence-matrix.md#a1-ultimate-oneness); disagreement register §1.

### E. Strongest objections

- **Judaism:** Flattening Jewish God-language into a generic “monotheism” erases covenant particularity and anti-idolatry tied to **YHWH–Israel**.
- **Christianity:** Separating “ultimate God” from **incarnate Christ** as merely optional “model” conflicts with normative Trinitarian **Religious tradition**.
- **Islam:** *Tawḥīd* is not generic monotheism; partnership/sonship language is **theologically rejected**, not bracketed.

### F. What remains distinct

Trinity and incarnation; Qur’anic polemic on sonship; Jewish halakhic life under **brit** with one God of Israel—not a philosophical “source of existence” abstraction alone. “Jewish divine simplicity” as a **technical** contrast to Trinity is **post-chronicle** vocabulary—use only as matrix-style contrast, not as verified Second Temple self-description.

### G. Sources

[Ch.1–2](../history/02-from-israel-to-judaism.md#provisional-comparative-significance-for-later-phases); [Ch.3 provisional table](../history/03-the-emergence-of-christianity.md#provisional-significance-for-later-comparison); [Ch.4 signposts](../history/04-the-emergence-of-islam.md#signposts-for-later-comparison-not-synthesis); `yahwism-monotheism-gradual`; `early-christ-devotion-hurtado`; `rabbinic-judaism-post-70-preview`; matrix disagreement §1; [source audit § A–B](./convergence-source-audit.md#a-jewish-theology-after-the-second-temple-convergence-claims).

## 2. Abraham and shared ancestral traditions

### A. Proposition

**Abraham/Ibrāhīm** functions across traditions as a **symbol of trust in one God and rejection of idolatry**, enabling limited common reference without a single historical biography or covenant line.

### B. Evidence by tradition

| Tradition | Content |
|-----------|---------|
| **Judaism** | Patriarch in Torah; covenant via Isaac/Jacob (**Religious tradition**). Historical Abraham **not** established ([Ch.1](../history/01-before-the-branches.md), `abraham-historicity`). |
| **Christianity** | Abrahamic faith; gentile inclusion re-reads Abraham ([Ch.3](../history/03-the-emergence-of-christianity.md)). |
| **Islam** | Ibrāhīm *ḥanīf*, Ka‘ba, prayer ([Ch.4](../history/04-the-emergence-of-islam.md)). |

### C. Historical relationship

**Shared inheritance** (name/figure in literary lineage); **Different interpretation** (Isaac vs. Ishmael priority, Mecca vs. land promises)—matrix § B.

### D. Compatibility test

**Translation-dependent convergence** on “Abraham as anti-idolatry archetype” (essay-style). **Irreducible disagreement** on **covenant heir**, sacrifice narratives, and ritual geography if proposition implies one story.

### E. Strongest objections

- **Judaism:** Ishmael/Isaac and **brit** specificity cannot be treated as optional.
- **Christianity:** Abraham faith **through Christ** in normative theology—not neutral archetype.
- **Islam:** Ibrāhīm and **Ka‘ba** are not interchangeable with Genesis alone.

### F. What remains distinct

Chosen line, land, hajj vs. temple; historical unknowability of patriarchs.

### G. Sources

[Ch.1 merge candidates](../history/01-before-the-branches.md#potential-merge-candidates-provisional-only); matrix § B; `abraham-historicity`.

## 3. Prophecy and continuity of revelation

### A. Proposition

God communicates through **prophets** across history, and later communities **inherit and reinterpret** earlier prophetic memory—without all traditions accepting the same prophetic **finality** or the same revelations as equally binding.

### B. Evidence by tradition

| Tradition | Content |
|-----------|---------|
| **Judaism** | Hebrew Bible prophets (Second Temple memory, **Ch.2**). **Cessation of prophecy** in **rabbinic self-understanding**: **Widely accepted interpretation** (Cohen, *Maccabees to Mishnah* — Ch.2–3 bib.), **not** a chronicle narrative (`rabbinic-judaism-post-70-preview`). |
| **Christianity** | Jesus as prophet/Messiah; apostles; Spirit ([Ch.3](../history/03-the-emergence-of-christianity.md)). |
| **Islam** | Muhammad seal of prophets (Q. 33:40, **Ch.4** § I; `quran-33-40-seal-prophets`); Qur’anic retelling of earlier figures. Exegesis and sectarian memory **not uniform** (Ch.4 § I). |

### C. Historical relationship

**Shared inheritance** (prophetic figures in overlapping texts); **Historical contact** (Christianity from Jewish milieu; Qur’an engages biblical lore); **Direct disagreement** on **finality** (Muhammad vs. Christ vs. no new prophet).

### D. Compatibility test

**Qualified convergence** on “prophetic moral critique and monotheistic proclamation exist in shared memory.” **Irreducible disagreement** on **cumulative revelation ending** or **decisive revelation in Christ/Qur’an**.

### E. Strongest objections

- **Judaism:** Prophecy **ceased**; Muhammad/Jesus claims not in Torah canon.
- **Christianity:** **Decisive** revelation in Christ conflicts with neutral “lineage.”
- **Islam:** **Finality** of Qur’an/Muhammad is not one episode in an open series.

### F. What remains distinct

Final prophet claims; canon boundaries; Jesus’ resurrection faith vs. Qur’anic 4:157.

### G. Sources

Matrix § F; disagreement §§3–4; [Ch.4 § I](../history/04-the-emergence-of-islam.md#i-the-formation-of-islamic-identity-and-authority); `quran-33-40-seal-prophets`; `rabbinic-judaism-post-70-preview`; [source audit § A, C](./convergence-source-audit.md).

## 4. Ethical obligations, justice, mercy, and care for others

### A. Proposition

Traditions affirm **moral accountability**, **care for the vulnerable** (poor, stranger), and **critique of unjust power**—expressed in law, prophecy, and eschatological judgment language.

### B. Evidence by tradition

| Tradition | Content |
|-----------|---------|
| **Judaism** | Prophetic ethics, covenant curses, Torah legal strands ([Ch.1–2](../history/01-before-the-branches.md)). |
| **Christianity** | Kingdom ethics, love command, table fellowship themes ([Ch.3](../history/03-the-emergence-of-christianity.md)). |
| **Islam** | Qur’anic accountability, zakāt, care for poor ([Ch.4](../history/04-the-emergence-of-islam.md)). |

### C. Historical relationship

**Shared inheritance** (ancient Near Eastern and biblical prophetic idiom); **Structural similarity** (virtue language); **Different interpretation** (Torah mitzvot vs. Pauline gentile debate vs. pillars of Islam).

### D. Compatibility test

**Qualified convergence** at **high level** (“accountability + mercy motifs + concern for weak”). **Documented motifs** (e.g. widow/orphan/stranger in Torah/prophetic rhetoric — [Ch.1 merge candidates](../history/01-before-the-branches.md#potential-merge-candidates-provisional-only); Meccan accountability — **Ch.4**) have **direct** textual/chronicle support; treating them as one **identical ethical system** is **broad comparative interpretation** only. **Not genuine convergence** if framed as same grounds of obligation (covenant law vs. grace vs. submission).

### E. Strongest objections

- **Judaism:** Halakhic **particularism** (Sabbath, kashrut) is not optional “shared ethics.”
- **Christianity:** **Christological** centre reshapes ethics; not deducible from generic justice list.
- **Islam:** Five pillars and sharī‘a **trajectory** not reducible to prophetic conscience alone.

### F. What remains distinct

Binding law details; role of Jesus’ death; Islamic ritual obligations.

### G. Sources

[Ch.1 ledger](../history/01-before-the-branches.md#potential-merge-candidates-provisional-only); matrix § C (ethics row), § H; `unified-covenant-ethics-os-proposal`; [source audit § D](./convergence-source-audit.md#d-shared-ethics-and-philosophical-convergence); essay III parallels tested below.

## 5. Revelation, scripture, and interpretation

### A. Proposition

Each tradition treats **scripture** as religiously authoritative while **interpretation** develops over time; historians can study composition and manuscripts without settling theological status.

### B. Evidence by tradition

| Tradition | Content |
|-----------|---------|
| **Judaism** | Torah centrality Second Temple; scribal transmission (**Ch.2**). |
| **Christianity** | Hebrew Bible + emerging NT; no closed canon early 2nd c. (**Ch.3**). |
| **Islam** | Qur’an 7th c.; oral/written transmission; standardization debate (**Ch.4**). |

### C. Historical relationship

**Shared inheritance** (Christian use of Jewish scripture); **Historical contact**; **Direct disagreement** on corruption/finality/inspiration claims (matrix § D, disagreement §6).

### D. Compatibility test

**Qualified convergence** on **methodological** distinction (wording vs. composition vs. community interpretation vs. theological status)—matches essay V in part. **Irreducible disagreement** on whether any text is **finally** revealed or **superseded**.

### E. Strongest objections

- **Judaism:** Oral Torah and **brit** bindingness lost in “library” model.
- **Christianity:** **Inspired NT** and Christ as interpretive key not neutral layers.
- **Islam:** **Inimitable, preserved Qur’an** not one layer among equals.

### F. What remains distinct

Canon lists; taḥrīf vs. inspiration; Christ/Qur’an as final word.

### G. Sources

Matrix § D; `quran-birmingham-parchment-rc`; `corinthians-15-pre-pauline-creed` (method analog); disagreement §6.

## 6. Law, grace, mercy, repentance, and obedience

### A. Proposition

Religious life combines **obligation** (law/command), **mercy/repentance**, and **human fallibility**—but the **centre of gravity** differs (Torah, Christ/grace, islam/submission).

### B. Evidence by tradition

| Tradition | Content |
|-----------|---------|
| **Judaism** | Torah, covenant fidelity, repentance themes (**Ch.2**). |
| **Christianity** | Paul on faith, gentiles, Torah scope (**Ch.3**); not Reformation caricature. |
| **Islam** | Accountability, mercy motifs; not Pauline grace parallel (**Ch.4** ledger). |

### C. Historical relationship

**Structural similarity** (shared vocabulary); **Different interpretation**; essay IV “three dimensions” is **philosophical proposal**, not historical equivalence (matrix § I).

### D. Compatibility test

**Translation-dependent convergence** if “law / mercy / submission” are **abstract philosophical dimensions**. **Irreducible disagreement** if proposition claims **doctrinal equivalence** of salvation/obligation.

### E. Strongest objections

- **Judaism:** **Mitzvot** are not “structure” beside optional mercy.
- **Christianity:** **Grace and Christ** are not one third of a generic triad.
- **Islam:** **Islam** (submission) is total orientation, not one leg of a stool.

### F. What remains distinct

Salvation mechanics; gentile Torah debate; sharī‘a development.

### G. Sources

[Ch.3 § F](../history/03-the-emergence-of-christianity.md#f-paul-the-gentile-mission-and-the-question-of-torah); [Ch.4 ledger](../history/04-the-emergence-of-islam.md#provisional-significance-for-later-comparison); matrix § C, § I.

## 7. Human responsibility, judgment, and ultimate destiny

### A. Proposition

Human beings face **moral accountability** and **judgment** themes; traditions use **resurrection/judgment** language with **limited** shared eschatological vocabulary but **disputed** details and soteriology.

### B. Evidence by tradition

| Tradition | Content |
|-----------|---------|
| **Judaism** | Resurrection debates Second Temple (Pharisees/Sadducees); judgment motifs (**Ch.2–3**). |
| **Christianity** | Easter faith; resurrection proclamation (**Ch.3**); event not verified by historians. |
| **Islam** | Paradise/hell, judgment scenes (**Ch.4**); Q. 4:157 affects passion narrative. |

### C. Historical relationship

**Shared inheritance** (Late Antique eschatology); **Direct disagreement** on Jesus’ death/resurrection and paths to salvation.

### D. Compatibility test

**Qualified convergence** on “accountability + judgment seriousness + epistemic limits about afterlife” (essay VI **eschatological humility**). **Irreducible disagreement** on **one path** or **same resurrection narrative**.

### E. Strongest objections

- **Judaism:** No Easter; messianic/eschatological schemes diverse.
- **Christianity:** **Resurrection of Jesus** is not bracketable for normative faith.
- **Islam:** Qur’anic eschatology and 4:157 are not generic “humility.”

### F. What remains distinct

Cross/resurrection; Qur’anic portrait of ‘Īsā; works/faith/grace debates.

### G. Sources

Matrix § I; disagreement §2 layers A–D; [Ch.3 § D](../history/03-the-emergence-of-christianity.md#d-resurrection-belief-and-the-birth-of-the-movement).

## 8. Covenant, community, and belonging

### A. Proposition

**Communal belonging** tied to God—Israel, church, *umma*—involves ritual, narrative, and obligation; **historical coexistence** is possible without **theological** mutual recognition.

### B. Evidence by tradition

| Tradition | Content |
|-----------|---------|
| **Judaism** | Peoplehood, brit, mitzvot (**Ch.2**). |
| **Christianity** | *Ekklesia*, baptism, gentile debate (**Ch.3**). |
| **Islam** | *Umma*, shahāda, early tribal-religious mix (**Ch.4**). |

### C. Historical relationship

**Different interpretation** (brit ≠ church ≠ umma); **Historical contact** (parting of ways; conquests); matrix disagreement §5.

### D. Compatibility test

**Qualified convergence** on “communities form identity through story + practice + God-orientation.” **Irreducible disagreement** on **exclusive valid covenant** claims.

### E. Strongest objections

- **Judaism:** **Election** not generic community.
- **Christianity:** **Body of Christ** and baptism into Christ.
- **Islam:** **Umma** under Qur’an and prophetic finality.

### F. What remains distinct

Conversion rules; Christ vs. Torah centrality; caliphal identity.

### G. Sources

`parting-of-ways-gradual`; matrix § G; disagreement §5.

## 9. Jesus and his place in religious history

### A. Proposition (split into sub-claims)

| # | Sub-proposition |
|---|-----------------|
| 9a | Historically: Jewish figure, crucified under Pilate (**Well established**, **Ch.3**). |
| 9b | Theologically (Christian **Religious tradition**): risen Lord/Messiah, centre of salvation. |
| 9c | Theologically (Qur’anic **Religious tradition**): honoured messenger/Messiah; Q. 4:157 denies crucifixion by foes. |
| 9d | Theologically (**normative Jewish streams after parting of ways**, not every 1st-c. Jew): Jesus not accepted as Messiah; no Easter faith (`jewish-rejection-jesus-messiah-normative`; Cohen, Boyarin, Lieu — Ch.3 bib.). |

### B–G (summary)

- **Relationship:** 9a **Shared inheritance** of historical method; 9b–9d **Direct disagreement** (register §2).
- **Compatibility:** 9a = **Genuine convergence** among historians using chronicle. 9b–9d together = **Irreducible disagreement** for same-sense theological narrative.
- **Objections:** Christian—denying resurrection empties faith; Muslim—affirming crucifixion contradicts Qur’an; Jewish—messianic claims rejected.
- **Distinct:** Incarnation, Trinity, 4:157, halakhic life without Christ.
- **Sources:** `jesus-crucifixion-pilate`; `quran-4-157-crucifixion`; `early-christ-devotion-hurtado`; `jewish-rejection-jesus-messiah-normative`; matrix § E; disagreement §2; [source audit § B, C](./convergence-source-audit.md).
