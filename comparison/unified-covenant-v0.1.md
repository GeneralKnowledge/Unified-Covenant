# Unified Covenant v0.1 — First Constructive Proposal

**Status:** Draft constitution for critical examination.  
**Not** a final creed, a declaration of religious truth, or a claim that Judaism, Christianity, and Islam already teach one identical religion.

**Grounding:** [history/01](../history/01-before-the-branches.md)–[04](../history/04-the-emergence-of-islam.md); [evidence-matrix.md](./evidence-matrix.md); [convergence-assessment.md](./convergence-assessment.md).  
**Preserved for comparison:** [THE_UNIFIED_COVENANT.md](../THE_UNIFIED_COVENANT.md) (original speculative essay, unchanged).

---

## Preamble

The Jewish, Christian, and Islamic traditions are considered together in this project because they share **identifiable historical contact and literary inheritance**: Israelite and biblical idiom, figures such as Abraham and Moses, prophetic moral rhetoric, monotheistic devotion in overlapping periods, and centuries of interaction—from the Jesus movement within Second Temple Judaism to the Qur’an’s engagement with Jewish and Christian communities in seventh-century Arabia. The four-chapter chronicle traces how **distinct communities** formed, not how one religion secretly split into three identical theologies.

Shared ancestry does **not** imply shared theology. The same name (*Abraham*, *Mūsā*, *ʿĪsā*) can carry **different interpretation**; “one God” can mean **structurally similar** rhetoric with **direct disagreement** on Trinity, incarnation, and *tawḥīd*; covenant language applies to **Israel**, the **church**, and the **umma** in ways the evidence matrix does not equate. The convergence assessment treats many surface similarities as **qualified** or **translation-dependent**, not as doctrinal equivalence.

This proposal therefore remains **honest about historical uncertainty** (contested historicity of patriarchs and exodus, limits of sira and Gospel sources) and about **religious disagreement** recorded in the disagreement register (crucifixion/resurrection vs. Q. 4:157, final revelation, prophethood). It does not treat historical relationship as proof of theological agreement, nor historical uncertainty as proof that a tradition’s claims are false.

The Unified Covenant v0.1 attempts what a comparative survey alone cannot: a **recognisable, internally coherent worldview** with explicit commitments on God, humanity, revelation, ethics, authority, and communal life—while **labelling** each commitment as common ground (Level A), deliberate constructive interpretation (Level B), or honestly unresolved (Level C). It is a **philosophical constitution inspired by** the Abrahamic traditions, produced by an intellectual project **without religious authority**.

## Part I — Constitutional principles

Each principle below is substantive. Levels follow the convergence assessment: Level A only where convergence is **genuine** or **qualified** without pretending identical metaphysics or soteriology; Level B where the constitution **adopts** a proposal the traditions do not uniformly teach; Level C where incompatible normative claims are **recorded, not harmonised**.

### UC-01 — One ultimate God (minimal monotheism)

**Level A — Common-ground commitment**

**Commitment:** Affirm **one ultimate God**—not a polytheistic pantheon of rival deities— as the orientation of devotion in all three traditions’ scriptural and early-historical layers, **without** requiring agreement on Trinity, incarnation, or identical metaphysics.

| Tradition | Relevance |
|-----------|-----------|
| **Judaism** | YHWH as God of Israel; exclusive devotion in many post-exilic texts; monolatry→monotheism **contested** in pace (Ch.1–2). |
| **Christianity** | One God of Israel; early exalted Christ-devotion in the 1st c. without Nicene formula (Ch.3). |
| **Islam** | *Tawḥīd*; rejection of *shirk* (Ch.4). |

**Basis:** Convergence assessment §1 (**Qualified convergence**); matrix § A1; disagreement register §1 (Trinity **irreducible** if same-sense metaphysics required).

**Qualifications:** Jewish covenant particularity, Christian Trinitarian **Religious tradition**, and Islamic rejection of divine sonship are **not** erased. “One ultimate God” here is **minimal**, not a shared creed formula.

---

### UC-02 — Moral accountability

**Level A — Common-ground commitment**

**Commitment:** Human beings are **morally accountable**—conduct matters, repentance and moral improvement are valued across traditions, and no ruler, institution, or nation is exempt from moral critique in principle.

| Tradition | Relevance |
|-----------|-----------|
| **Judaism** | Covenant fidelity, prophetic lawsuit, Torah legal strands (Ch.1–2). |
| **Christianity** | Kingdom ethics, Pauline accountability; resurrection faith as divine vindication (Ch.3). |
| **Islam** | Qur’anic accountability, judgment scenes (Ch.4). |

**Basis:** Convergence assessment §§4, 7 (**Qualified**); matrix §§ C, I; Ch.1 merge candidates (accountability of rulers).

**Qualifications:** **Grounds** of obligation differ (mitzvot, Christ/grace, islam/submission). This principle does **not** claim identical anthropology, sin, or grace doctrines.

---

### UC-03 — Justice with mercy (paired, not fused)

**Level A — Common-ground commitment**

**Commitment:** **Justice and mercy belong together** in religious moral imagination: punishment without proportion and compassion, mercy without responsibility, and law without humanity are each treated as **incomplete** in prophetic and legal rhetoric across traditions.

| Tradition | Relevance |
|-----------|-----------|
| **Judaism** | Prophetic critique; covenant curses and restoration; legal care strands (Ch.1–2). |
| **Christianity** | Mercy commands; Paul’s tension of faith and obligation (Ch.3). |
| **Islam** | Mercy (*raḥma*) and accountability motifs (Ch.4); not Pauline “grace” parallel (Ch.4 ledger). |

**Basis:** Convergence assessment §4; matrix § I (mercy language **structural similarity**); essay III tested as **partial overlap** only.

**Qualifications:** Does **not** derive one code of law. Particular halakha, Christological ethics, and pillars of Islam remain distinct.

---

### UC-04 — Care for the vulnerable

**Level A — Common-ground commitment**

**Commitment:** Protect the **poor, the stranger, the widow, the orphan**, and others at social margin—obligations expressed in Torah legal/prophetic idiom, Christian kingdom/table ethics, and Qur’anic concern for the weak.

| Tradition | Relevance |
|-----------|-----------|
| **Judaism** | Ch.1 merge candidates; prophetic and legal strands (Ch.1–2). |
| **Christianity** | Kingdom reversal themes; collections for poor (Ch.3). |
| **Islam** | Zakāt and Meccan accountability rhetoric (Ch.4). |

**Basis:** Convergence assessment §4 (documented **motifs**); Ch.1 [potential merge candidates](../history/01-before-the-branches.md#potential-merge-candidates-provisional-only); matrix § H (alms).

**Qualifications:** Application, scope, and theological **why** differ. Modern human-rights language is **not** claimed as traditional common ground.

---

### UC-05 — Prophetic moral critique in shared memory

**Level A — Common-ground commitment (qualified)**

**Commitment:** God is understood to address communities through **prophets** who confront injustice, idolatry, and abuse of power; later traditions **inherit and reinterpret** that prophetic memory.

| Tradition | Relevance |
|-----------|-----------|
| **Judaism** | Hebrew Bible prophets; Second Temple memory (Ch.2). |
| **Christianity** | Jesus as prophetic figure; apostolic proclamation (Ch.3). |
| **Islam** | Qur’anic retelling of Mūsā and others; Muhammad as preacher (Ch.4). |

**Basis:** Convergence assessment §3 (**Qualified**); matrix § F.

**Qualifications:** **Finality** conflicts (Muhammad seal of prophets, Christ as decisive revelation, rabbinic cessation of prophecy **attributed**, not chronicle-narrated). No neutral “open prophecy” doctrine.

---

### UC-06 — Historical baseline on Jesus of Nazareth

**Level A — Common-ground commitment (historical only)

**Commitment:** Accept for constitutional purposes the **historian’s minimal consensus** where the chronicle supports it: Jesus was a **Jewish** figure active in Galilee/Judea, executed by **crucifixion under Roman authority** (Pontius Pilate).

| Tradition | Relevance |
|-----------|-----------|
| **Judaism** | Later rejection of messianic claims does not negate historical execution (**Well established** in Ch.3). |
| **Christianity** | Aligns with passion narratives and external witnesses (Ch.3). |
| **Islam** | Qur’an presupposes Jesus; **theological** denial of crucifixion (Q. 4:157) is **not** this sub-commitment (see UC-C02). |

**Basis:** Convergence assessment §9a (**Genuine convergence** among historians); matrix § E; `jesus-crucifixion-pilate`; disagreement register §2 layers.

**Qualifications:** **Theological** claims about resurrection, messiahship, and Q. 4:157 are **Level C**, not included here.

---

### UC-07 — Eschatological humility

**Level A — Common-ground commitment (qualified)**

**Commitment:** Affirm the **seriousness** of moral accountability and judgment themes while **not** adopting any single tradition’s detailed soteriology or afterlife map as constitutional truth.

| Tradition | Relevance |
|-----------|-----------|
| **Judaism** | Diverse Second Temple eschatology; judgment motifs (Ch.2–3). |
| **Christianity** | Easter faith central to **Religious tradition**—bracketed here at constitutional level (Ch.3). |
| **Islam** | Paradise/hell in Qur’an (Ch.4). |

**Basis:** Convergence assessment §7; essay VI (**Supported** as qualified); matrix § I.

**Qualifications:** Christians for whom resurrection is non-negotiable may treat this as **membership tension** with v0.1’s constitutional bracketing—not as agreement with their creed.

---

### UC-08 — Fourfold claim discipline (interpretive method)

**Level B — Constructive interpretation**

**Proposal:** All constitutional and interpretive work must distinguish: (1) **historical claims** open to evidence; (2) **scriptural claims** requiring interpretation within communities; (3) **theological commitments** adopted by this covenant; (4) **questions left open** (Level C).

| Tradition | What it takes |
|-----------|----------------|
| **Judaism** | Scribal transmission, composition history of Torah (Ch.2). |
| **Christianity** | Gospel source criticism; pre-Pauline formulas as tradition history (Ch.3). |
| **Islam** | Qur’an as 7th-c. corpus; sira/hadith limits (Ch.4). |

**New assumptions:** A **meta-epistemology** for the project: historians document belief without verifying supernatural causation (Ch.3 § D; Ch.4).

**Likely rejection:** Communities that subordinate historical study to exclusive revelation may reject (2) as applied to their canon.

**Why adopted:** Convergence assessment §5 (**Qualified** on **method**); matrix § D “what historians can study.”

---

### UC-09 — Layered sacred library (no harmonised mega-canon)

**Level B — Constructive interpretation**

**Proposal:** Study texts in **layers**—shared narrative inheritance, tradition-specific corpora (Tanakh, NT, Qur’an), and the **interpretive record** (scholarship, disagreement, manuscript history)—without declaring all texts equally authoritative in the same sense or rewriting them into one book.

**New assumptions:** Constitutional study prioritises **visibility of disagreement** over single closure.

**Likely rejection:** Normative claims of **inspired/final** scripture in each tradition (disagreement register §§3, 6).

**Why adopted:** Essay V tested as **Qualified method**; convergence assessment §5.

---

### UC-10 — Non-coercive coexistence and dissent

**Level B — Constructive interpretation**

**Proposal:** **Political and civic coexistence** among religious communities without compulsory theological agreement is a constitutional ideal; **theological pluralism** (all paths equally true in the same sense) is **explicitly rejected** as Level C incompatibility with exclusive **Religious tradition** claims.

| Tradition | What it takes |
|-----------|----------------|
| **Judaism** | Diaspora experience under empires (Ch.2). |
| **Christianity** | Minority in empire (Ch.3). |
| **Islam** | Patchwork post-conquest; not mass conversion (Ch.4 § H; `conquest-not-mass-conversion`). |

**New assumptions:** Modern constitutional **freedom of conscience** beyond pre-modern legal practice.

**Likely rejection:** Communities holding exclusive revelation may reject only the **non-coercion** limb if they understand mission or law as divinely mandated.

**Why adopted:** Convergence assessment §10a (**Qualified** on coexistence); §10b **Irreducible** on theological equivalence.

---

### UC-11 — Accountability of religious authority

**Level B — Constructive interpretation**

**Proposal:** **Religious authority** (text, institution, scholar, conscience) is legitimate only insofar as its **moral and interpretive claims remain examinable**—no interpretation is immune merely because it is ancient, powerful, or sincerely held.

**New assumptions:** Explicit **anti-clerical** constitutional norm (essay III “honest inquiry”).

**Likely rejection:** Traditions treating oral Torah, magisterium, or *ijmāʿ* as binding without external critique.

**Why adopted:** Partial overlap with Ch.1 merge candidate “fallibility”; essay III; **not** historical convergence as unified traditional doctrine.

---

### UC-12 — Revision, ledger, and non-sacred synthesis

**Level B — Constructive interpretation**

**Proposal:** This constitution is **versioned** (v0.1), **traceable** (claim ledger below), and **revisable** when evidence or argument improves; the Unified Covenant as a project **is not sacred** merely because it synthesises earlier traditions.

**New assumptions:** Institutional design from essay VII (council, archive, dissent)—**philosophical**, not historical (convergence assessment §10c).

**Why adopted:** Enables Part II constitution without pretending historical religions already taught this meta-rule.

## Part III — Positive worldview

### A. God and ultimate reality

**Affirm (Level A):** One ultimate God in the **minimal** sense (UC-01)—creator/sustainer language shared as **structural similarity**, with **Religious tradition** specifying nature differently.

**Do not affirm as common ground:** Trinity, incarnation, or *tawḥīd* as identical propositions. These are **Level C** (UC-C01).

**Constructive stance (Level B):** The covenant **brackets** metaphysical adjudication at the constitutional level while allowing participants to state their tradition’s **Religious tradition** claims in parallel registers—not as mutually cancelled “models.”

### B. Humanity and moral responsibility

Humans are **accountable agents** (UC-02), capable of wrongdoing, repentance, and improvement, with **justice-mercy pairing** (UC-03). **Belief and conduct** both matter in each tradition, but **relationship** differs: Torah observance, faith-in-Christ soteriologies, and islam/submission are **not** declared equivalent (convergence §6 **Translation-dependent** only if abstracted—here left distinct).

### C. Revelation and truth

**Level A:** Prophetic memory and moral seriousness in scripture (UC-05, UC-08).  
**Level B:** Layered library (UC-09); cumulative **study** without cumulative **theological finality**.  
**Level C:** Which revelation is **final** or **uncorrupted** (UC-C03, UC-C06).

Historical investigation may support or challenge **composition** claims; it does **not** by itself prove or disprove **Religious tradition** assertions of divine origin (matrix § I supernatural claims).

### D. Ethics and obligations

Practical framework for participants: **justice with mercy** (UC-03), **protection of vulnerable** (UC-04), **honesty** in inquiry (UC-11), **limits on coercion** (UC-10). Obligations are **binding within this covenant** as constitutional ethics, not as replacement halakha, canon law, or sharī‘a. Shared **motifs** do not imply shared **legal derivation** (convergence §4).

### E. Community and religious difference

**Membership** (Part II) is **covenantal participation in the project**, not merger of **brit**, **ekklesia**, and **umma** (matrix § G; convergence §8). Each historical institution retains its own identity; v0.1 seeks **coexistence and dialogue**, not one superseding community.

### F. Authority and interpretation

Authority flows from: **scripture (within traditions)**, **historical scholarship**, **philosophical argument**, **inherited interpretation**, and **individual conscience**—with **UC-11** requiring examinable justification. Disputes escalate to **disagreement procedure**; Level C blocks false consensus.

## Part VI — Challenges to the first proposal

### 1. Privileging modern liberal ethics over inherited authority

**Objection:** UC-10, UC-11, and UC-04 smuggle **modern constitutional liberalism** into “Abrahamic” ethics, overriding traditions that ground obligation in revelation, not examinable public reason.

**Response:** v0.1 **labels** these as Level B with **new assumptions**; it does not claim they are traditional common ground.  
**Vulnerability:** Participants seeking **pure** traditional derivation will still see the covenant as **normatively foreign**.

### 2. Shared ancestry without unified theology

**Objection:** The project’s very premise overreads **historical contact** as meaningful **theological** convergence (convergence summary: many themes **Qualified** only).

**Response:** Level A is **minimal**; Level C is large; essay’s overreach (archetype tables, law/grace triad) is **not** promoted to Level A.  
**Vulnerability:** Readers may skim Level A and ignore qualifications—**communication risk** remains.

### 3. Minimal monotheism misrepresenting Trinitarian Christianity

**Objection:** UC-01’s “one God” without Trinity **misrepresents** normative Christianity, where God **is** Father, Son, and Spirit—not a bracketed minimum.

**Response:** Constitution distinguishes **participation pact** from **Christian creed**; Level C records Trinity.  
**Vulnerability:** Many Christians cannot adopt even **procedural** bracketing without feeling the proposal is **theologically inadequate**.

### 4. Historical criticism vs. revelation

**Objection:** UC-06 and UC-08 treat crucifixion as **constitutional historical fact**, conflicting with **Religious tradition** readings of Q. 4:157 and with fideistic epistemologies.

**Response:** **Epistemic lanes** (historical vs. theological) explicit; disagreement register §2 layers honoured.  
**Vulnerability:** Single-lane epistemologies **reject the split**—unresolved.

### 5. Failing exclusive-revelation communities

**Objection:** Communities whose **Religious tradition** requires decisive revelation (Christ, Qur’an, Torah) cannot treat UC-07, UC-09, UC-C03 as **live and open** without betraying their faith.

**Response:** Membership is **dialogue pact**, not replacement religion; Level C honesty preferred to false merge.  
**Vulnerability:** **No compromise** may satisfy exclusivists; covenant may be ** irrelevant** to them.

### 6. Ethical similarity concealing different foundations

**Objection:** UC-03 and UC-04 create **illusion** of one ethics while **why** obligations bind differs (covenant law, Christ, submission).

**Response:** Qualifications on every Level A ethical principle; convergence §4 **not genuine** for identical grounds.  
**Vulnerability:** Short-form use still **overstates** unity.
