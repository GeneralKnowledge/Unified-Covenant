## 1. Chosen identity: a unified religion

Unified Covenant (UC) v0.2 is a **deliberate attempt** to articulate one coherent religious worldview that:

1. **Inherits** the Abrahamic scriptural streams and their interpretive traditions as **revelatory witnesses**, not as decorative quotations.
2. **Preserves revelation wherever possible** (§2) before adopting novel doctrine or recording irreconcilable conflict.
3. **Makes explicit commitments** where inherited claims cannot be reconciled without abandoning meaningful content from at least one stream.
4. **Remains revisable** under §10 while functioning as a **real religion** for adherents—not merely a research consortium.

UC is **not** Judaism, Christianity, or Islam. Adherents may have come from any of those paths; UC **does not** claim to be the “true” form of any single inherited religion.

---

## 2. Governing principle: preserve revelation wherever possible

When traditions appear to contradict, UC applies this **sequence** (also operationalized in §4):

1. **Represent faithfully** — State what each relevant text, tradition, or normative stream asserts; separate primary wording from later theology and popular summaries ([matrix disagreement register](./evidence-matrix.md#direct-disagreements-and-unresolved-incompatibilities)).
2. **Search interpretive diversity** — Language, genre, translation, historical setting, and internal schools (e.g. Q. 4:157 exegesis: **Contested** per matrix gap register; Nicene Christology **outside** Ch.3).
3. **Test reconciliation seriously** — Ask whether readings can coexist without forcing a source to mean what its normative holders reject.
4. **Preserve strongest defensible readings** — Prefer interpretations that retain revelatory content over convenience harmonisation.
5. **Test coherence** — Cross-check against wider texts, UC principles, and historical evidence (including **H** baselines).
6. **Record the result** — **Reconciled**, **provisionally reconciled**, **unresolved**, or **irreconcilable** (under current evidence).
7. **Make the religious decision explicit** — Where UC must teach something, state **recommended doctrine**, **alternatives**, and **theological cost**.

**Limits:** Preserving revelation is **not** asserting every incompatible claim is simultaneously true in the same sense. UC may **reinterpret**, **layer**, or **choose** among inherited claims when irreconcilability persists—always labelled as **UC doctrine (B)**, not as universal Abrahamic consensus.

## 3. Three kinds of authority

| Kind | Role in UC | Typical sources in this repository |
|------|------------|-----------------------------------|
| **Historical evidence (H)** | Constrains UC **historical narrative**, public teaching about past events, and comparative claims labelled **Well established** / **Widely accepted interpretation** | Chronicle, registry (e.g. `jesus-crucifixion-pilate`), matrix historical layers |
| **Inherited revelation and tradition (R)** | What scriptures and normative streams **claim** about God, ethics, and salvation—including **Religious tradition** labels and internal diversity | Tanakh, NT, Qur’an as cited in Ch.1–4; matrix **Religious tradition** cells |
| **Unified Covenant doctrine (B)** | What **this religion affirms, reinterprets, combines, rejects, or leaves open** for members | This document; future adopted revisions |

**Conflict rule (summary):** For **past public events**, **H** binds UC teaching unless a future revision documents new historical consensus. For **theological truth**, **R** streams are heard at full strength in reconciliation (§2); **B** decides membership doctrine when reconciliation fails. **H does not automatically falsify R** (Ch.3 § D; matrix § I); **R does not erase H** (UC-C02).

## 4. Method and epistemic hierarchy

### 4.1 Claim classification (revised)

| Label | Meaning | Membership |
|-------|---------|------------|
| **A — Inherited convergence** | Motif or commitment **documented** across relevant streams at **high level**, with **non-identical** grounds and meanings unless proven otherwise (convergence **Qualified** or **Genuine** where noted) | Usually **motif affirmation**; not full theology |
| **B — Unified Covenant doctrine** | **New religious commitment** adopted by UC | **Essential** or **recommended** per principle table |
| **H — Historical baseline** | Conclusion of historical inquiry adopted for UC **narrative and catechesis**; **not** shared interfaith creed | Required for UC **historical teaching**; distinguished from Muslim/Christian **R** claims on same topic |
| **C — Contested doctrine** | Live disagreement; reconciliation incomplete or **irreconcilable** under defensible readings | **Recorded**; may be **essential UC choice** (see docket) or **open dissent zone** |

Principles may contain **multiple labels**; v0.2 **splits** v0.1 entries where one label hid mixed claims (UC-01, UC-05, UC-06, UC-07, UC-10).

### 4.2 Decision procedure (when domains collide)

**Priority for theological synthesis:** (1) **Preserve R** via §2 sequence → (2) **Philosophical coherence** of proposed **B** → (3) **Ethical implications** of UC constitution → (4) **H** for **historical description only** → (5) **Faith commitments** where evidence underdetermines (marked **provisional**).

**Not equal weight:** UC does **not** treat all three canons as automatically decisive in identical ways (see §7). **H** does **not** override **R** in confessional registers (private prayer, tradition-specific study groups); **H** **does** bind **official UC historical curriculum** and statements prefixed “as history.”

**Fourfold discipline (from UC-08, retained and corrected):** Classify every disputed proposition as **historical (H)**, **scriptural/interpreting (R)**, **UC doctrinal (B)**, or **intentionally open (C)** before debate proceeds.

**Default for joint UC texts:** Theological articles of faith follow **B**; appendices on history follow **H**; comparative sidebars cite **A** motifs with qualifications.

## 5. Declaration of faith (UC v0.2 — proposed)

*Short form for catechesis; expanded rationale in §§6–9.*

We affirm **One God**—Sovereign, Merciful, and Just—who creates, guides, and judges humanity; who spoke through the prophets of Israel, through Jesus the Anointed, and through Muhammad; who binds us in **covenant responsibility** toward God and neighbour.

We receive the **Tanakh**, the **New Testament**, and the **Qur’an** as **layered revelatory witnesses** in the Unified Canon (§7), interpreted under UC discipline—not as interchangeable texts of identical authority, but as one **family of witness** requiring honest reconciliation.

We hold that **conduct matters**, that **repentance** is always possible before judgment, and that **justice and mercy** must not be separated in religious life.

We accept the **historical crucifixion** of Jesus under Roman authority as part of our shared historical memory (H), and we affirm **divine vindication of Jesus** after that event (B, provisionally reconciled with Christian resurrection faith and Qur’anic protection of ‘Īsā—see UC-C02).

We honour **Muhammad** as prophet in the Abrahamic succession and accept **prophetic finality in the sense of UC** (§8)—not as a claim that no divine word ever occurs, but that no new canonical covenant replaces the Unified Canon without UC revision.

We await **judgment and the life to come**, trust in God’s ultimate mercy without denying accountability, and commit to **peaceful coexistence** with those who do not adhere—without claiming that all paths are theologically equivalent.

### 7.3 Revelation continuity (B)

God’s **word** is understood as **continuous across the series**—Israelite prophets, Christ, Muhammad—without erasing **distinct missions**. **Finality (UC sense):** No further **canonical** covenant document beyond the Unified Canon layers + UC Constitution unless **§10 revision** adds one—**provisionally reconciled** with Islamic finality language and **in tension** with Christian “decisive revelation in Christ” (UC-C03, UC-C04).
