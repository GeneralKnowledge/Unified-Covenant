## 1. Arabic text and grammar

### 1.1 Core clause (Q 4:157)

Standard Uthmanic consonantal text (reading with common vowels):

> **wa mā qatalūhu wa mā ṣalabūhu wa lākin shubbiha lahum**

| Element | Notes |
|---------|--------|
| **wa mā qatalūhu** | “And they did not kill him” — negated verb *qatala* (kill/slay), 3rd masc. pl. subject (context: *yahūd*, “the Jews,” in surrounding verses). |
| **wa mā ṣalabūhu** | “And they did not crucify him” — negated *ṣalaba* (crucify). |
| **wa lākin** | “But rather / however” — adversative. |
| **shubbiha lahum** | Passive of Form II *shabbaha* (“to make resemble / make appear like”); **lahum** = “to them” or “for them.” |

**Adjacent verses (Q 4:158):** *bal rafaʿahu llāhu ilayhi* — “Rather, God raised him up to Himself” (translation varies: “exalted him,” “raised him to Himself”).

**Broader context (Q 4:155–158):** Polemic against *kufr*; charges include killing prophets, slander against Mary, and **boasting** that they killed the Messiah (*al-masīḥ ʿīsā ibn maryam*). Crucifixion language appears in refuting that boast, not as a standalone passion narrative.

### 1.2 Plausible translations (not interchangeable theologically)

1. **Standard Muslim-majority reading:** “They neither killed nor crucified him, but it was made to appear so to them” → denial that Jesus was successfully killed/crucified by his foes (often extended to **no crucifixion of Jesus at all**).
2. **Agency-focused reading (minority):** “They did not kill/crucify him” = they lacked **effective agency**; event may still occur under divine permission (compare Lawson’s distinction between “Jews did not crucify” vs. “Jesus was not crucified” — see §3).
3. **Appearance-only reading:** Emphasis on *shubbiha* — wrong **perception** of who died or what happened, without the verse specifying mechanism (substitution developed later in tafsīr).

**Grammar note:** *Shubbiha* does **not** by itself name a substitute; substitution is **exegetical supply** (Mevorach 2023; Lawson).

## 4. Substitution, non-substitution, and divine agency

**Arguments for substitution (T):**

- Explains *shubbiha lahum* as “someone else looked like Jesus.”
- Supported by early isnād chains in Ṭabarī (Mevorach 2023).

**Arguments against substitution as Qur’anic literal meaning:**

- Verse does not name a substitute; Form II passive may concern **appearance to observers**, not identity swap (Mevorach; Lawson).
- Substitution stories often classified *Isrāʾīliyyāt* — external narrative material (Lawson).

**Non-substitution denial readings:**

- Jesus never crucified; taken up to heaven (majority popular Muslim teaching; Lawson introduction).

**Divine agency / vindication (Q 4:158):**

- *Rafaʿa* — exaltation/raising — widely linked to **divine vindication** of ʿĪsā regardless of crucifixion debate.
- **Christian parallel (R):** resurrection/exaltation kerygma (1 Cor 15; `corinthians-15-pre-pauline-creed`) — **different narrative**, comparable **theological function** (vindication) only at abstract level.

## 6. UC v0.2 “divine vindication” reading — precedent vs novel synthesis

**v0.2 position (UC):** Affirm **H** crucifixion; affirm **B** “God vindicated Jesus after the cross”; read 4:157 as denial that **enemies defeated God’s purpose**, while accepting Roman execution historically.

| Question | Assessment |
|----------|------------|
| Identifiable in classical tafsīr as **majority** view? | **No** — majority denies crucifixion of Jesus (Mevorach 2023; Lawson on majority). |
| Partial precedents? | **Yes, partial:** (1) Q 4:158 exaltation; (2) agency readings (Lawson); (3) minority Gospel-harmony views (Ismaʿīlī; Khorchide — **disputed**). |
| Identifiable in Christian **R**? | **Resurrection/vindication** yes; **with** crucifixion as saving event — **not** UC’s split of H crucifixion + purpose-denial read of 4:157. |
| **Classification** | **UC synthesis — plausible but contested**; **not** “supported” as inherited Muslim-Christian consensus. |

**Does it preserve strongest defensible readings?**

- **Preserves H** crucifixion (strong).
- **Preserves** Qur’anic **exaltation** motif (4:158).
- **Does not preserve** literal denial-of-crucifixion reading dominant in **R** Islam.
- **Does not preserve** Nicene “crucified-and-raised for salvation” as unified narrative.

**Verdict:** Largely **avoids explicit contradiction** by splitting H and theological registers; **does not** fully preserve normative **R** content on both sides — **theological cost** (see [decision docket](../comparison/unified-covenant-v0.2-decision-docket.md) MD-02).
