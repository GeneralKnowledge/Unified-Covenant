## 1. Executive summary

Phase 6.5 investigates whether and how Judaism, Christianity, and Islam understand the **preservation**, **alteration**, **concealment**, and **misinterpretation** of earlier scriptures, with primary weight on **Qur’anic** vocabulary (*ḥarrafa*, *yuharrifūn*, related charges) and its **exegetical history**.

**Strongest defensible findings:**

1. **Lexical and contextual diversity:** Qur’anic roots from *ḥ-r-f* appear in **several distinct accusatory contexts**—misplacing words from their proper settings (e.g. Q 4:46; 5:13), concealing or mixing truth with falsehood (Q 2:42; 3:187), writing with hands what is falsely attributed to God (Q 2:79), neglecting or breaking covenant (Q 5:13–15), and **legal/theological rivalry** over judgment (Q 5:41–48). **Not every occurrence supports one uniform doctrine** of wholesale physical corruption of the Torah or Gospel (**H** on usage; **R** on later systematisation).

2. **Classical exegetical split:** Major **classical** tafsīr (notably al-Ṭabarī on several key verses, per recent specialist analysis) often emphasises **distortion of meaning**, **misapplication**, or **concealment** more than **replacement of entire written archetypes**—while **other** exegetes, **ḥadīth** reports, and **popular Muslim teaching** frequently assert **textual** alteration or loss (**R** diversity; **C** for UC adjudication).

3. **Academic revisionist strand:** Several scholars (e.g. Reynolds 2010; Di Matteo 1922 tradition; recent philological studies) argue **Qur’anic** *taḥrīf* language aligns closely with **anti-Jewish Christian polemic** about **misinterpretation** and **misrepresentation**, and that **“textual falsification of the Bible”** as a **systematic Islamic doctrine** **developed later** in polemical literature (**H**/**contested** reconstruction—not a Muslim **R** consensus).

4. **Jewish and Christian preservation claims:** Judaism and Christianity **do not** typically claim **letter-for-letter immutability of every manuscript**; they claim **providential preservation** of **scripture as canonically received** (Masoretic / Vulgate / Byzantine traditions; **R**), while **H** textual criticism documents **variants**, **translation layers**, and **canon-development** without automatically refuting **R** theology (**C** at theology–history interface).

5. **UC canon tension (unchanged status):** v0.3 **receives** Tanakh, NT, and Qur’an as **Unified Canon** while **DR-10 / UQ-01** leave **preservation theology** **C**. That combination is **coherent** if UC treats layers as **witnesses under CCRP** without a **B** ruling that any layer **wholly fails** to transmit earlier revelation—but **operationally fragile** if members need **explicit guidance** on whether to **privilege** one layer’s historical-critical verdict over another’s **R** preservation claim. Phase 6.5 **does not** resolve this; it maps costs (§10).

**Recommendation pointer:** See [options memo](./phase-6-5-tahrif-options.md). **Maintainer recommendation:** **Option A** (keep **MD-06**) remains **defensible** given evidence gaps and **R** diversity; **Option C** is the **strongest candidate** if the community later wants **B** without adopting wholesale polemic—**not adopted here**.

## 2. Definitions and terminology

| Term | Notes | Typical classification |
|------|--------|-------------------------|
| ***Taḥrīf*** (Arabic *ḥ-r-f*) | “To turn aside, deviate, distort”; in Qur’anic discourse often **verb** *yuḥarrifūn(a)* | Context-dependent; **not** one fixed English equivalent |
| ***Taḥrīf al-maʿnā*** | Distortion of **meaning**, misinterpretation, misapplication | **R** (Islamic taxonomy); argued as **primary Qur’anic** sense by some **H** scholars |
| ***Taḥrīf al-naṣṣ*** | Alteration of **written text** | **R** (Islamic polemic/law); popular **R**; debated as **Qur’anic** default |
| **Concealment / kitmān** | Hiding revelation (Q 2:42; 3:187; 5:15) | Often **oral/legal** or **selective disclosure**, not only erasure |
| ***Tawrat* / *Zabūr* / *Injīl*** | Qur’anic designations for revelation associated with Moses, David, Jesus | **R** (Qur’an); **C** mapping to modern Jewish/Christian **canon lists** |
| **People of the Book (*ahl al-kitāb*)** | Jews and Christians (and sometimes subgroups) in Qur’anic address | **R**; historical **H** on 7th-c. communities **contested** |
| **Masoretic Text (MT)** | Standard Hebrew Bible consonantal text + vocalisation tradition | **H** artifact; **R** authority for rabbinic Judaism |
| **Septuagint (LXX)** | Greek Jewish scripture tradition | **H**; **R** for some churches historically |
| **Textual criticism** | Reconstruction of earliest recoverable wording from manuscripts | **H** |
| **Inspiration / inerrancy** | God’s role in scripture’s truthfulness | **R** (Christian); varieties **C** internally |
| **Oral Torah (*Torah she-be-‘al peh*)** | Rabbinic claim of Sinai-origin oral instruction | **R** Jewish; **not** UC binding per v0.3 §5.1 |

**UC project rule (MD-06):** Manuscript **H** **does not alone** prove or disprove **R** preservation or *taḥrīf* (**B** not set).

## 3. Qur’anic passage-by-passage analysis

Method per passage: (1) Arabic wording (transliteration summary), (2) context, (3) what is explicitly identified as distorted/concealed, (4) what is **not** established, (5) classical tafsīr tendency, (6) modern scholarship, (7) internal Muslim diversity, (8) domain (text / oral / interpretation / law).

**Translation note:** English renderings vary (“distort,” “alter,” “change,” “shift words from their contexts”). Classification below uses **Arberry-style** glosses only where needed; maintainers should cite one **fixed UC study translation** for CCRP.

### 3.1 Q 2:75–79 (Banī Isrā’īl narrative block)

**Arabic (core lemmas):** Q 2:75 — *afatamannawna … anna ta’manū qawman … qad ātaakum Allāhu ḥukman wa kitāban … wa minhum ummatun yaslūna kalāma Allāhi wa yuḥarrifūnahu min baʿdi mā ʿaqalūhu wa hum yaʿlamūn*; Q 2:78–79 — *ummiyyūn* … *yaktubūna al-kitāba bi-aydīhim … li-yashtarū bihi thamanan qalīlan*.

| # | Analysis |
|---|----------|
| **1. Wording** | *Yuḥarrifūnahu* = “they alter/distort **it**” (object: *kalām Allāh* / what they heard). Q 2:79: writing *al-kitāb* with hands, falsely attributing to God. |
| **2. Context** | Mid–Sūrat al-Baqara polemic against Banī Isrā’īl; follows stories of calf, covenant-breaking; anticipates 2:79’s “woe to those who write.” |
| **3. Explicit charge** | A **party** heard **God’s word** then **distorted it after understanding**; some **illiterate** rely on **forged writing** for gain. |
| **4. Not established** | That **entire Torah scrolls** in 7th-c. Jewish possession were **physically replaced**; that **Christians** are primary target; exact referent of *ummiyyūn* (unlettered Jews vs. others) — **C**. |
| **5. Classical tafsīr** | al-Ṭabarī (per UIN Sunan Kalijaga 2024 thesis summary): **emphasis on maʿnā** — wrong halakhic/moral application after comprehension, **not** necessarily rewriting Torah consonants. al-Rāzī, Ibn Kathīr trad.: mix of **textual** and **meaning** readings (comparator sites; **abstract/summary level** in this phase). |
| **6. Modern scholarship** | Reynolds 2010: Qur’anic idiom parallels **Christian anti-Jewish** “misuse of scripture”; semantic field **misrepresentation**. Philological article (Mofidu 2024): argues **Qur’anic** *ḥ-r-f* **≠** textual falsification in four key verses (**contested**). |
| **7. Muslim diversity** | Popular preaching often **textual**; reformist/modern apologetics sometimes **meaning-only** to defend interfaith dialogue. |
| **8. Domain** | **Primarily:** interpretation, **concealment**, **fabricated legal writing**; **secondarily** (in some tafsīr): **lexical** change. |

### 3.2 Q 3:78

**Arabic:** *wa minhum lafẓun … yuḥarrifūna l-lisāna fī l-kitābi li-yaḥsabū … ghayra mā huwa fī l-kitābi*.

| # | Analysis |
|---|----------|
| **1–2** | Sūrat Āl ʿImrān; **People of the Book** altering **tongue/speech** regarding **the Book** so hearers misunderstand. |
| **3** | **Oral performative** distortion — how scripture is **recited/explained**, not necessarily ink on parchment. |
| **4** | Does **not** name **which** book (Torah vs. Gospel vs. both); **C**. |
| **5–7** | Classical: often **tahdīd al-lisān** (twisting tongue) — **R** exegetical category **tahrif al-qawl**; modern: Reynolds layer **“shift words from contexts.”** |
| **8** | **Oral / interpretive** > textual. |

### 3.3 Q 4:46

**Arabic:** *min alladhīna hādū yuḥarrifūna l-kalima ʿan mawāḍiʿihi wa yaqūlūna … wa yuḥarrifūna l-kalima ʿan mawāḍiʿihi*.

| # | Analysis |
|---|----------|
| **1–2** | Address to **those who became Jews** (*hādū*); legal-polemical context (Mā’ida parallels). |
| **3** | **Explicit:** moving **the word** from its **proper places** (*mawāḍiʿ*) — idiomatic for **misquoting**, **punning**, **selective citation**. |
| **4** | Does **not** assert **loss of entire Injīl**; linked in tafsīr to **specific** halakhic disputes (e.g. “**raʿinā**” wordplay). |
| **5** | al-Ṭabarī (thesis summary): **verbal** distortion without endorsing ** wholesale textual** swap. |
| **6** | Reynolds: prototype of **non-textual** *taḥrīf* in Qur’an. |
| **7** | Some combine with **global** corruption narratives via **ḥadīth** chains — **R** not **Qur’an-explicit**. |
| **8** | **Interpretation / legal rhetoric**. |

### 3.4 Q 5:13–15

**Arabic (13):** *bima nabaṯa ʿan al-mīthāq … ghayyarū … nassan min baʿdi mawāḍiʿihi*; **(14–15):** Gospel (*injīl*) brought by Jesus; People of the Book **concealed** (*yaktumūn*) what Muhammad recites; **clear proofs** came to them.

| # | Analysis |
|---|----------|
| **1–2** | Sūrat al-Mā’ida; **covenant breach** → **changing words from their places**; then **Injīl** and **concealment** of Muhammad’s message. |
| **3** | **Covenant violation**; **lexical relocation** (*ghayyarū nassan*); **kitmān** of **new** prophetic witness — **not identical** charges. |
| **4** | **Injīl** here = **revelation to Jesus** (**R**); mapping to **canonical Gospels** is **exegetical** (**C**). |
| **5** | al-Ṭabarī on 5:13 (thesis): may **sound textual** but often read as **wrong writing/interpretation** presented as Torah. |
| **6** | Academic: **5:15** supports **continuity** — “**clear proofs** came to them from their Lord” implies **accessible** prior revelation (**A** motif at high level only: **scripture still bears witness** — **not** identical theology). |
| **7** | Muslim apologetics: **5:15** proves **Bible still contains truth**; polemicists: **only** before **total** corruption — **internal tension**. |
| **8** | **Mixed:** covenant **H** memory; **textual/interpretive** *ghayyarū*; **concealment** of **Muḥammadan** proclamation. |

### 3.5 Q 5:41

**Arabic:** *yuharrifūna l-kalima min baʿdi mawāḍiʿihi* in context of **hasty judgment**, **bribes**, **turning away** when Muhammad judges.

| # | Analysis |
|---|----------|
| **1–2** | **Legal-polemical:** Jews (in dominant reading) **distort** scripture to **evade** Muhammad’s adjudication. |
| **3** | **Misplacement of words** + **bad faith** in law — parallel to 4:46. |
| **5** | al-Ṭabarī (thesis): **disobedience** to Torah/Muḥammadan ruling, **not** text swap. |
| **8** | **Legal application / interpretation**. |

### 3.6 Q 5:43–48 (judgment, Torah, Gospel, Qur’an)

**Arabic (47–48):** *wa anzilnā ilayka l-kitāba bi-l-ḥaqq … wa kitāban anzalnāhu ilayka … li-tuḥkima baynahum … wa anzilnā l-tawrāta wa l-injīla … hudan wa nūran … li-aḥkama l-nabiyyūna … wa katabnā ʿalayhim … wa anzalnā l-furqāna*.

| # | Analysis |
|---|----------|
| **1–2** | Rhetorical **why ask Muhammad to judge** when **Torah** has **God’s judgment**; affirmation that **Torah and Injīl** were **sent as guidance and light**; **Prophets judged by them**; **Qur’an** as **furqān** confirming prior revelation. |
| **3** | **Explicit positive:** earlier **kitāb** **valid** as **source of judgment** in principle; **Qur’an** **confirms** and **guards** (*muhaymin*) — Q 5:48. |
| **4** | Does **not** say **current** Jewish/Christian **codices** are **identical** to **original** revelation; does **not** deny **partial** distortion charges elsewhere — **tension** within Qur’an (**C** for harmonisation). |
| **5–7** | Classical: **abrogation** debates vs. **confirmation**; modern Muslim **Biblical reliability** apologists lean on 5:43–48; **textual corruption** advocates stress **other** verses — **R** split. |
| **8** | **Theology of continuity** + **jurisprudence**; **presupposes** **some** readable/accessed **Torah/Injīl**. |

### 3.7 Other materially relevant passages (brief)

| Passage | Contribution |
|---------|----------------|
| **Q 2:42, 2:174, 3:187** | **Concealment** (*yaktumūn*) of what was revealed — **kitmān** strand distinct from *ḥ-r-f*. |
| **Q 7:162, 7:169** | Alteration **after** knowledge — parallel **ethical** charge. |
| **Q 6:91** | Jews **not** treating **revealed book** with due honour — **neglect**, not necessarily **erasure**. |
| **Q 10:94** | Command to **ask** People of the Book if **in doubt** — implies **some** authoritative **consultation** (**C** with polemic passages). |
| **Q 4:157–158** | **Passion** dispute (Phase 6.3); **separate** from *taḥrīf* lexicon but affects **Injīl** trust (**C**). |

### 4.2 Chronological sketch (**H** / **R** mixed)

1. **Qur’anic discourse (7th c. CE, **R** text):** Mixed charges — **misplacement of words**, **concealment**, **covenant breach**, **positive** affirmation of **Tawrat/Injīl** as **guidance**.  
2. **Formative exegesis (8th–10th c.):** Systematic tafsīr; **ḥadīth** insertion; **debates** on **Jewish** vs. **Christian** targets.  
3. **High medieval polemic (e.g. Andalusian):** Sharper **anti-Christian** & **anti-Jewish** **R** claims; **philosophical** engagement (Ibn Rushd — **not** surveyed deeply here).  
4. **Modernity:** **Apologetics**, **print Bibles**, **missionary** debate → **textual** *taḥrīf* **popularised**; **reformist** **maʿnā**-only readings; **academic** **Qur’anic studies** (Reynolds, Griffith, Lawson) **re-contextualise**.

### 4.3 Qur’anic book-names vs. modern canons (**C**)

| Qur’anic term | Common Muslim **R** mapping | Jewish / Christian **R** mismatch risk |
|---------------|------------------------------|----------------------------------------|
| **Tawrat** | Torah of Moses | Often **Pentateuch** only — not full **Tanakh** |
| **Zabūr** | Psalms of David | **Ketuvim** subset |
| **Injīl** | Revelation to **Jesus** | **Not** = four Gospels **simpliciter** in **R** Islam; **Christian R** = **canon** + **Tradition** |

## 5. Jewish preservation and textual-transmission analysis

### 5.1 **R** claims (internal diversity noted)

- **Torah** from Sinai — **written** + **oral** (Mishnah Avot 1:1 chain — `mishnah-avot-1-1-torah-chain`).  
- **Masoretic** stream as **authorised** Hebrew text (**R** rabbinic; **H** development 7th–10th c. CE vocalisation).  
- **Scribal precision** (*soferim*, *tiqqun soferim* — **R** categories for **small** deliberate orthographic/theological adjustments — **contested H** whether “adjustments” are **later** rabbinic **R**).

### 5.2 **H** evidence (selected)

| Topic | **H** finding | Does **not** imply |
|-------|---------------|---------------------|
| **Dead Sea Scrolls** | Pre-Masoretic **Hebrew** diversity; **shared** much with MT | Automatic **invalidation** of **R** Torah |
| **Septuagint** | Alternate **vorlage**; longer **Jeremiah**, etc. | That **Hellenistic Jews** lacked **scripture** |
| **MT vs. DSS** | Mostly **close** for Torah; **variants** elsewhere | **Wholesale** **medieval** **fabrication** |
| **Canon formation** | **Tanakh** **closed** gradually (**H** **contested** dates) | **Single** **R** timeline across **Judaism** |

### 5.3 Interface (**C**)

**R** preservation asserts **God’s** fidelity to **covenant people** through **transmission process**; **H** documents **human** copying variance. **Orthodox** Judaism often **integrates** both; **critical** Judaism may **prioritise** **H** without **R** inerrancy — **internal** **C**.

**UC:** Oral Torah **not** UC binding (v0.3 §5.1) — **preservation** debate for UC focuses on **Tanakh** **layer**, not **full** rabbinic **R** package.
